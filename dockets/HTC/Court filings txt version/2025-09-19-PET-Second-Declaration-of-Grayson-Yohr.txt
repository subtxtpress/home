My name is Grayson Yohr. I am the employee of the petitioner in the matter. I am writing this second declaration to provide the court with additional information regarding the actions of the respondent and the impact it has had on Lauren Matthias and those around her.

The effect that the Respondent and her stalking and harassing behavior has had on Lauren has been extreme. As things have escalated, I have noticed a severe mental health decline in Lauren. I am usually with Lauren for 12 or more hours a day. The two of us also travel frequently for work, and during travel, we are together nearly 24 hours a day.

Aside from her husband, I am the one person she spends the most time with and I have been the person she has confided in the most during this, as to the effects it has had on her mental health. I was the person with Lauren on the day she was confronted by the respondent in April. I was also the person who alerted Lauren on June 19th that people “wanted her head on a platter,” after being told that on a phone call.

After speaking with police that day, the officer called two people who we had direct information were involved. The officer told us one of the individuals implicated the respondent. We never knew that he had in-fact called the respondent until she posted a video mocking the officer, which is marked as exhibit 9-Police video.

Just a few days after we called the police over safety concerns, a video popped up on my TikTok for you page made by the respondent. After clicking the profile, I quickly realized that the respondent had made an entire profile dedicated to harassing Lauren. I was the one who informed Lauren about this. The shift in Lauren’s mental health became even more noticeable after the public social media attacks began. I have seen the panic and fear in Lauren over the last few months, as the respondent continues her pattern of escalation.

I have had numerous conversations with Lauren about the effect this conduct has had on her mental health. On multiple occasions during those conversations she has expressed that she did not want to continue living because of how difficult this experience has been. I have spent many hours with Lauren listening to her, helping her process events, and assisting her in looking for ways to stop the respondent’s escalation. I have also spent significant time speaking with John about how best to support Lauren.

One of the most difficult days was July 7th, when John came to me over his concern for Lauren and her expression of wanting to kill herself. That same morning Lauren had texted me saying she had a panic attack. Later that day, she expressed concern over John’s health to me over text. (Exhibit 40-Concerns)

John’s mental health has also declined as a result of the respondent’s conduct. He frequently tells me how stressed he feels and that he is unable to complete work tasks I have asked of him because there is simply too much happening. When Lauren and I are out of town, he often calls me just to confirm that nothing has occurred and that we are safe.

The respondent’s conduct has also had a significant impact on Lauren and John’s eight-year-old son. I frequently spend time with him and take him on outings, and he has confided in me that he has overheard conversations about what is happening. He has told me that he feels sad and that he is afraid when we leave town. Although every effort is made to limit such discussions in his presence, it has been nearly impossible to shield him completely, as the respondent’s behavior occurs at all hours. Each incident creates an immediate disruption, filling the home with fear and preventing the family from continuing with normal activities. Over time, I have also observed changes in his behavior: he has become more irritable, and more tearful. His appetite has decreased, and he has grown increasingly attached to Lauren—often needing to be at her side when she is at home and often refusing to let any other adult in the home help him with basic things, aside from her.

Lauren, John, and I work closely together in their family business. Before the respondent’s stalking and harassment began, both Lauren and John were able to devote much more time

and focus to their work. We regularly held weekly meetings to plan projects and divide responsibilities. Since the respondent’s conduct started, those meetings have largely shifted away from business planning and instead center on how everyone is coping and how to keep the business functioning despite the constant harassment and fear created by the respondent and her accomplices.

After the Court granted the temporary civil stalking injunction on September 4, I hoped that Lauren and I would finally feel safe attending upcoming work events. However, within mere hours, the respondent violated the order, and since then she has continued to violate it repeatedly.

From September 5–7th, we attended the CrimeCon expo in Aurora, Colorado. After arriving on Thursday, September 4th, Lauren and I contacted local law enforcement to notify them of the injunction. That evening the respondent posted a TikTok which is titled “Whoops Video” in exhibits.

The following morning, Lauren and I met with officers, as well as with the head of CrimeCon security. During that meeting, we were informed that the respondent herself had already spoken with CrimeCon staff about the injunction and had essentially indicated that she intended to disregard it.

To say both Lauren and I had an immediate increase in fear, is an understatement. We missed the first few hours of CrimeCon while we waited for the Aurora Police Department to ensure she was removed. Law enforcement notified us later that day that the respondent had been personally served a physical copy of the order and was removed from CrimeCon.

Just hours later, the respondent was standing only a few feet away from Lauren and me in the bar area of the hotel with only socks on, and holding a camera in her hand. Lauren and I had already been in the bar for several hours when I first saw the Respondent. Lauren told me she was afraid, but she also expressed fear of being followed if we left the bar area. I asked a friend nearby to keep an eye on Lauren while I sought help with another friend. I first asked hotel staff if there were any officers nearby. When I was told there were not, I returned to the bar and called an officer with the Aurora Police Department. The respondent remained in the bar for quite some time, but left shortly after noticing I was on the phone. She then made two more videos on her targeted social media accounts within just a couple of hours after leaving the bar. When Lauren and I saw them, Lauren expressed extreme fear due to the threatening nature of the videos.

The next afternoon, I was seated in the hotel bar area having lunch with Lauren and several colleagues when both of us observed the respondent just a few feet away. She was escorted into the adjoining restaurant located directly behind us. This restaurant is part of the same indoor bar and dining area and is well within 500 feet of where we were seated.

Based on my observation, the distance was closer to approximately 75 feet, which constituted another violation of the order.

The fact that she continues to defy the order, even after it was signed by a judge, is extremely frightening. Her repeated violations demonstrate not only a complete disregard for the authority of this Court, but also a willingness to escalate her behavior regardless of the legal consequences. This makes it impossible for us to feel safe, as we are left to wonder when and how she will next attempt to insert herself into our lives. It shows that standard boundaries and legal directives do not deter her, leaving us in a constant state of fear and vigilance. The ongoing defiance underscores the seriousness of her conduct and the urgent need for strong and continued protection from the Court.

For months, the three of us have attempted to move forward and disregard the respondent’s behavior. However, it has become impossible to ignore, as it continually disrupts our core sense of safety and relationships with those around us. We live in a constant state of fear, losing sleep and bracing ourselves for what the respondent may do next. The conduct has only escalated over time and has never de-escalated.

Lauren and her husband deserve to perform their jobs without fear of being stalked and harassed through multiple avenues. Lauren deserves to be able to attend work functions out of state without fear of who may show up and harm her. She shouldn’t have to be panicked twenty-four hours a day about what the respondent may do next, and how it can impact her life, the life of her family and her family’s livelihood. The respondent has shown she will go to great lengths to harm Lauren and seemingly anyone in a close proximity to her.

For all of these reasons, I respectfully ask the Court to grant a permanent civil stalking injunction. The respondent’s conduct has not diminished over time but has only escalated, even in the face of judicial orders. This ongoing pattern of harassment and intimidation has stripped Lauren, John, their young son, and all of us who work closely with them of any sense of security in our daily lives and professional activities. Without lasting protection, Lauren will continue to live in constant fear for her safety and the safety of her family. A permanent injunction is the  only measure that can provide the security and peace of mind necessary to allow them to move  forward with their lives and their work without the continual shadow of fear created by the respondent’s actions.
