Lauren Matthias, Host of YouTube's Hidden True Crime, Fights to Keep Dismissed Stalking Injunction on Utah Creator Christina McArthur's Record 

A year after dropping a case that she initiated, Matthias's new filing continues to identify MacArthur as a stalker. Matthias asks the court to consider MacArthur's public video content as evidence against her despite the judge having already said that the original order was "in no way meant to silence" McArthur. 

PROVO, Utah (October 2026): Lauren Matthias, host of YouTube's Hidden True Crime channel, filed an objection to Christina MacArthur's petition to expunge a temporary stalking order that Matthias herself asked the court to dismiss in October 2025. 

September 4, 2025: The district court issued the temporary order ex parte meaning that MacArthur had no opportunity to respond. At the September 22, 2025 hearing on whether the temporary order would be permanent, Matthias was not prepared to proceed in the time set aside for the hearing, and the Court continued the hearing until a later date. However, at the hearing, Judge Kasey Wright described the temporary stalking injunction as "a one-sided order that was initially signed that was based on one-sided information." Instead of proving her claims at the continued hearing, Matthias asked the Court to dismiss the injunction. The Court's October 14, 2025 order is plain: "The Court grants the motion and dismisses the temporary civil stalking injunction," and "the case is closed." Matthias never took the stand, nor were her claims ever tested under cross-examination.

"I asked for a hearing because I wanted to prove her accusations false. Two days before I could do that in court, she dismissed her own case. A year later, she's still calling me a stalker in court papers and using my videos as the reason to keep that label on me. The judge said this order was never meant to silence me. She's still actively trying to make it do exactly that."
Christina MacArthur

What happened: 
• Sept. 22, 2025: The hearing MacArthur requested began. The judge narrowed the order from the bench and said he "in no way intended" to keep her off social media. He continued the hearing to Oct. 16, 2025 because there wasn't enough time to hear every witness. MacArthur's side objected to the delay. 
• Oct. 14, 2025: At a hearing on Matthias's own motion to continue, her attorney moved to dismiss. The court granted the motion and dismissed the case 
• September 2026: MacArthur petitioned to expunge her record under Utah Code 78B-7-1003. 
• Oct. 26, 2026: Matthias objected. 

Why it matters: 

What does Matthias gain? The order was temporary, issued without hearing or any opportunity for MacArthur to submit evidence in her defense, and never made final. Expunging it would not erase the underlying facts: Utah law allows a court to consider the same circumstances in any future case (Utah Code 78B-7-1004(8). Matthias's stated reason for objecting is that expungement would let MacArthur answer as though the order never existed while continuing to talk publicly about the case. 

Double standards. Matthias built her channel covering trials and hearings that are open to the public, but in her own case, she wanted the court to close the October 16, 2025 hearing. On the morning of October 14, Judge Wright denied Matthias's motion, saying that "the presumption is openness to allow the public access to court proceedings to preserve transparency and public confidence in the justice system". He approved Law & Crime's request to cover the hearing. That afternoon, Matthias's attorney moved to dismiss the case. 

Matthias's objection is about speech. Matthias asks the court not to expunge MacArthur's record because she continues to talk about the case and about Matthias herself. Judge Wright, who issued the order, said it "was in no way meant to silence what Ms. MacArthur is doing." 
Case: Matthias v. MacArthur, No. 250404055, Fourth Judicial District Court, Provo. Judge Kasey L. Wright.
