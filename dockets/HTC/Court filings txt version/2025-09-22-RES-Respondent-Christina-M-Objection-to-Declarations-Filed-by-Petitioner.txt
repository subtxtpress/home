5. Second Yohr Declaration
a. Rules 401–402. The Second Yohr Declaration overlaps extensively with the Yohr Declaration and has many of the same evidentiary problems. Yohr brings up numerous events and communications in which Christina M did not participate. Yohr makes general statements like “Lauren and her husband deserve to perform their jobs without fear of being stalked and harassed through multiple avenues.” Such statements are irrelevant.
b. Rule 602. Yohr fails to establish foundation for many statements in the Second Yohr Declaration.  Again, the main issue is that Yohr lacks personal knowledge and attempts to lay foundation for testimony based only upon what Matthias told her. She also speculates about Christina M and others’ motives and what will happen if the injunction does not issue.
c. Rules 801–804. Yohr recites numerous out-of-court statements for their truth. Yohr does not lay foundation for any of the hearsay exceptions. Indeed, a substantial portion of Yohr’s testimony is not based on her personal observations, but, instead, what Matthias and others told her.
d. Rule 1002. Yohr makes broad statements about Christina M’s social media content as the basis for the requested stalking injunction. The Court should disregard these statements and, instead, require that Yohr produce the original or a duplicate to the extent allowed under Utah Rule of Evidence 1003.
6. N Declaration – The N Declaration is not actually a declaration because it is not signed and does not contain the verification language required under Utah Code § 78B-18a-106. The Court should disregard it in its entirety.

Page 6

a. Rules 401–402. N offers general statements regarding the alleged effects of cyber bullying and recounts the contents of conversations that she had with Matthias. N testimony is irrelevant given the elements Matthias must prove in this case.
b. Rule 602. N testimony is based primarily on statements from Matthias rather than her personal observations or knowledge. N testimony lacks foundation.
c. Rules 702–703. N purports to opine based on her experience in politics. N does not lay sufficient foundation to establish her expertise and does not even attempt to set forth her methodology. Any opinion offered by N is not reliable given that she is a “close friend” of Matthias and lacks objectivity and therefore credibility as an alleged expert. See Utah R. Evid. 702(b), 703. Finally, allowing expert testimony in this case would be prejudicial given the lack of procedural protections (e.g. discovery) for Christina M.
d. Rules 801–804. Because N testimony derives primarily from out-of-court statements that Matthias told N and that N is essentially repeating in her declaration, the N Declaration contains inadmissible hearsay to which no exception applies.
