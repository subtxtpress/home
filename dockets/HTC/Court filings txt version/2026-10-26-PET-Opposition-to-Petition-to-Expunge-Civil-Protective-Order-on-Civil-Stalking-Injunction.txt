Petitioner Lauren Matthias ("Petitioner"), by and through undersigned counsel, hereby opposes Respondent Christina MacArthur's ("Respondent") Petition to Expunge Civil Protective Order or Civil Stalking Injunction pursuant to Utah Code § 78B-7-1003(3)(b). Petitioner respectfully requests that the Court deny the Petition because Respondent has not established the statutory requirements for expungement and Petitioner has continued her conduct directed at Petitioner.

ARGUMENT

I. RESPONDENT HAS NOT SATISFIED THE REQUIREMENTS OF UTAH CODE § 78B-7-1003(5). 

Utah Code§ 78B-7-1003(5)(a) permits a court to expunge an ex parte civil protective order 

1 of 4

or civil stalking injunction only when the statutory requirements are satisfied. As relevant here,§ 78B-7-1003(5)(a)(i) requires that the ex parte  civil order be "dismissed, dissolved, or expired upon a hearing by the court." Here, Petitioner voluntarily dismissed the underlying proceeding before the scheduled hearing occurred. The Court therefore did not conduct the hearing contemplated by § 78B-7-1003(5)(a)(i), and the temporary order was not dismissed, dissolved, or expired upon a hearing by the Court. Respondent's Petition nevertheless represents that the temporary order ''was dismissed, dissolved, or expired after a hearing." That representation does not reflect the procedural history of this case because the matter was voluntarily dismissed before the hearing.

Section 78B-7-1003(5)(b) does not cure this deficiency. That subsection provides a separate pathway when the petitioner did not appear at the hearing. Here, there was no hearing at which Petitioner failed to appear; Petitioner and Respondent voluntarily dismissed the proceeding before the scheduled hearing. No evidentiary hearing on the request was held, and the Court made no determination on the merits. Accordingly, Respondent has not established that either statutory pathway for expungement of the ex parte civil order under § 78B-7-1003(5) applies. The Court should, therefore, deny Respondent's request.

II. EVEN IF THE STATUTORY REQUIREMENTS WERE MET, THE COURT SHOULD EXERCISE ITS DISCRETION TO DENY EXPUNGEMENT GIVEN RESPONDENT'S CONTINUED HARASSMENT OF PETITIONER.

Section 78B-7-1003(5) provides that a court "may" expunge an ex parte civil protective order when the listed conditions are satisfied. The statute is permissive, not mandatory. Satisfying its conditions makes an ex parte order eligible for expungement; it does not entitle the respondent to it.

The circumstances here warrant denial. In the year since the underlying proceeding was dismissed, Respondent has continued to direct conduct at Petitioner and her family, including, by way of example:

a) On May 29, 2026, at CrimeCon in Las Vegas, Respondent positioned herself with a tripod­ mounted camera near where Petitioner was meeting with event security and, after being directed to move, resumed filming Petitioner from her new location. Exhibit 1 and Exhibit 3.
b) On August 5, 2026, Respondent appeared on a publicly broadcast YouTube program and stated of Petitioner's husband, a licensed psychologist: "I can confidently say this this man has...he might have his license, but he is a fraud." Exhibit 2, Exhibit 3, Exhibit 4.
c) On July 24, 2026, Respondent published a video on her YouTube channel whose description includes the phrases "lauren matthias is the stalker," "lauren matthias stalking lies," and "hidden true crime doesnt have a stalker." Exhibit 5.

Respondent's July 24 publication makes the significance of expungement here concrete. The ex parte order in this case reflects the Court's finding that there was reason to believe stalking had occurred and that Respondent was the stalker. Respondent now publicly asserts that Petitioner is "the stalker." Expungement would cause the civil order to be "considered to never have occurred" and would permit Respondent to "reply to an inquiry on the matter as though there was never a civil order." Utah Code§ 78B-7-1004(3). Granting that relief while Respondent continues to publicly characterize these proceedings, and while she has stated she does not intend to stop, would not serve the purposes of the statute.

CONCLUSION

For the foregoing reasons, Petitioner respectfully requests that the Court deny Respondent's Petition to Expunge Civil Protective Order or Civil Stalking Injunction.
