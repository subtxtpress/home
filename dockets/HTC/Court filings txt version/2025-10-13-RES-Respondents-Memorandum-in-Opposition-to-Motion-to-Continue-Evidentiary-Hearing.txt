Respondent Christina M (“M”), through counsel and pursuant to Rule 7 of the Utah Rules of Civil Procedure, hereby files this memorandum in opposition to the Motion to Continue Evidentiary Hearing (the “Motion to Continue”).

RELIEF REQUESTED AND SUPPORTING GROUNDS

The Court should deny the Motion to Continue and hold the evidentiary hearing on October 16, 2025 (the “Hearing”), as scheduled. The Court already postponed the hearing

Page 1

nearly a month at the insistence of Petitioner Lauren Matthias (“Matthias”). In the meantime, Christina M has been subject to an Amended Temporary Restraining Order (the “Temporary Order”) with its accompanying limitations on Christina M’s civil rights. Matthias and her counsel have had ample time to prepare for the Hearing and cannot show “compelling reasons” for an additional continuance.

RELEVANT PROCEDURAL FACTS

1. Matthias filed this action on August 28, 2025. See Request for Civil Stalking Injunction (Dkt. No. 1).
2. Matthias served Christina M with the Temporary Order on September 5, 2025. See Return of Service (Dkt. No. 18).
3. Christina M requested a hearing, and the Court set the original date for the Hearing on September 17, 2025. See Docket Notice dated September 9, 2025.
4. Christina M retained counsel on September 11, 2025. See Notice of Appearance (Dkt. No. 23).
5. Matthias refused to stipulate to a continuance of the Hearing, and Christina M filed a motion to continue on September 12, 2025. See Motion to Continue Evidentiary Hearing (Dkt. No. 27).
6. The Court granted the motion to continue the Hearing and rescheduled it three business days later on September 22, 2025. See Docket Notice dated September 16, 2025.
7. The parties appeared on September 22, 2025 for what became a scheduling conference and partial evidentiary hearing (the “Initial Hearing”).
8. Matthias stated at the Initial Hearing that she was unprepared or unwilling to proceed by proffer to finish the full evidentiary hearing in the two hours the Court allotted for the Hearing.
9. Matthias insisted that the Court set the Hearing for at least a half-day, which resulted in the Court rescheduling the Hearing for October 16, 2025. See Docket Notice dated September 22, 2025.
10.  Matthias now claims that a continuance is warranted because her newly-retained counsel needs time to get up to speed, even though her prior counsel appears to be continuing as counsel of record. See generally Docket.
11. The Initial Hearing and the parties’ efforts since then have removed much of the “guesswork” inherent in these proceedings. For example, the parties have reached a stipulation regarding nearly all of the proposed exhibits.
12. Accordingly, bringing Matthias’s new counsel up to speed should be relatively simple.

ARGUMENT

Utah Code § 78B-7-701(5)(b)(i) provides that the court “shall hold a hearing requested by the respondent at the earliest possible time and within 10 days after the day on which the request is filed with the court unless the court finds compelling reasons to continue the hearing.” Here, Christina M respectfully requests that the Court deny the Motion to Continue and proceed as scheduled with the Hearing. Matthias cannot show “compelling reasons” for continuing the Hearing. Further, Christina M’s prejudice from further extending the Temporary Order outweighs any “reason” Mattias may have to request a continuance.

Page 2

A. Matthias Cannot Show Compelling Reasons to Continue the Hearing.

Matthias initiated this action and bears the burden to establish her entitlement to the requested injunction by a preponderance of the evidence. See Utah Code § 78B-7-701(5)(b)(ii). Matthias knew or should have known of this obligation and the truncated timetable under Utah’s Civil Stalking Statute. See id. § 78B-7-701(5)(b)(i). As the petitioner, Matthias had a significant “head start” in preparing to attempt to prove her case. By the time Matthias served Christina M, Christina M had just over a week to prepare. Nevertheless, Matthias refused to stipulate to a continuance to allow Christina M (and, really, both sides) to prepare for the Hearing. The Court granted Christina M’s requested continuance, but postponed the Hearing only three business days. Both sides were obligated to appear on September 22, 2025 to participate in the full evidentiary hearing on Matthias’s request for a permanent injunction.

Matthias’s insistence at the Initial Hearing prompted the Court to postpone the Hearing to October 16, 2025—nearly a month later. If Matthias believed that she needed to supplement her legal team, she could have done so immediately. The fact that Deiss Law entered its appearance just “six days before the evidentiary hearing” is solely a function of Matthias’s delay. See Motion to Continue at 1. Matthias gives no reason for the delay, much less a compelling reason that would justify the Court postponing the Hearing. 

Matthias points out that the Court has a pending request for press access to the Hearing and Matthias’s corresponding objection (the “Objection”). But, again, Matthias does not attempt to explain why the request and Objection should prompt the Court to postpone the Hearing. At the Initial Hearing, the Court heard and denied Matthias’s request to seal the courtroom. There is

Page 3

no reason that the Court cannot fully address the request and the Objection at the Hearing. Matthias attempts to use the request as a pretext for the desired continuance.

B. An Additional Continuance Prejudices Christina M’s Rights.

Matthias’s recent correspondence with and filings to the Court are replete with unfounded allegations against Christina M. See, e.g., Email from Matthias’s Counsel to Court Clerk, dated October 6, 2025, a true and correct copy of which is attached hereto as Exhibit A. Matthias claims that she feels threatened and unsafe by online posts about matters of public interest that are not directed towards Matthias. The evidentiary hearing must proceed to require Matthias to prove her accusations.  Similarly, Christina M is entitled to her day in Court to present her defenses. By the time of the Hearing, Christina M will have been subject to the Temporary Order for nearly a month and a half with its accompanying effect on Christina M’s civil rights. Matthias could and should have been prepared for the full evidentiary hearing at the time of the Initial Hearing. She has had almost a whole additional month. Christina M’s prejudice from the continued restriction on her civil rights far outweighs Matthias’s need for yet more time to prepare to prove claims that she should have been prepared to prove from the outset.

CONCLUSION

Matthias initiated this action and knew or should have known of her obligations to prove her claims by a preponderance of the evidence.  The Court patiently allowed Matthias almost an additional month after she was unprepared at the Initial Hearing.  Matthias could have hired Deiss Law to supplement her legal team at any time before or after the Initial Hearing. Christina M should not suffer ongoing prejudice due to Matthias’s choice to retain additional

counsel just “six days” before the Hearing.  Christina M respectfully requests that the Court deny the Motion to Continue and proceed with the Hearing as scheduled.
