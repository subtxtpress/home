II. LEGAL STANDARD

A.  Presumption and Authority to Close Hearings

While Utah courts operate under a general presumption of openness, this presumption is not absolute. Courts possess inherent authority to close proceedings when necessary to protect compelling interests, including victim safety and privacy.

Utah Code of Judicial Administration Rule 4-202.02(4)(S) specifically recognizes that records of hearings closed to the public are classified as private:
(i) permanently if the hearing is not traditionally open to the public and public access does not play a significant positive role in the process; or 
(ii) if the hearing is traditionally open to the public, until the judge determines it is possible to release the record without prejudice to the interests that justified the closure.

This rule acknowledges the Court's authority to close hearings when compelling interests outweigh the presumption of openness.

B.  Electronic Media Coverage

Utah Code of Judicial Administration Rule 4-401.01(2)(A) establishes a presumption that electronic media coverage shall be permitted in public proceedings. However, the judge may prohibit or restrict such coverage "if the judge finds that the reasons for doing so are sufficiently compelling to outweigh the presumption."

Page 3
 
Rule 4-401.01(2)(B) requires the judge to consider factors including:

•  The privacy rights of all parties and participants;
•  The difficulty of effectively concealing from the jury evidence, documents, or exhibits not admitted into evidence;
•  Whether coverage will cause harm to any participant;
•  Whether coverage will significantly distract participants or impair the dignity of the proceedings; and
•  Whether coverage will interfere with the fair administration of justice.

Rule 4-401.01(2)(C) mandates that if the judge prohibits or restricts coverage, "the judge shall make particularized findings orally or in writing on the record." Rule 4-401.01(2)(D) requires that "[a]ny reasons found sufficient to prohibit or restrict electronic media coverage shall relate to the specific circumstances of the proceeding rather than merely reflect generalized views or preferences."

C.  Balancing Test for Court Records

When determining whether to allow access to court records, Rule 4-202.04(6) requires the Court to: (A) make findings and conclusions about specific records; (B) identify and balance the interests favoring opening and closing the record; and (C) if the record is ordered closed, determine there are no reasonable alternatives to closure sufficient to protect the interests favoring closure. 

Rule 4-202 recognizes the following interests served by non-public court records: (1) To protect personal privacy; (2) To protect personal and public safety; and (3) To protect non-parties participating in the court process, such as victims and witnesses.

V. CONCLUSION

The Court should reject the unspecified, unverifiable media request. Utah's judicial administration rules recognize that some proceedings—particularly those involving victims of stalking, harassment, and abuse—require protection from public exposure.
Rule 4-202.02(4)(B)(iii) specifically classifies stalking injunction records as private.
Rule 4-202.02(4)(S) authorizes closure of hearings when the interests justifying closure would be prejudiced by release of the record.
Rule 4-401.01 permits prohibition of electronic media coverage when reasons are sufficiently compelling. This is precisely such a case.

Petitioner seeks legal protection from a stalker. Providing that protection requires Petitioner to testify about traumatic experiences and reveal sensitive details about location, routines, and safety measures. Requiring this testimony to occur in front of media representatives, subject to recording and broadcast, would place Petitioner and her family in immediate physical danger; re-traumatize the victim; chill full and truthful testimony; Enable Respondent's continued campaign of harassment; Transform a protective proceeding into a public spectacle; and serve no compelling public interest. 

The Court has both the authority and the obligation to protect stalking victims who seek its assistance. That protection must include shielding victims from forced public exposure of their trauma while they seek legal relief.
