11-21-2025
Judge Hightower issues an Order Denying Request for Continuance on November 21, 2025.

Presented before the Court is Defendant's Request for Continuance, filed November 19, 2025. Defendant generally asserts that certain items of discovery have not been turned over and that the case therefore cannot be ready for trial on January 5, 2026. The State filed its Response to Defendant's Motion for Continuance on November 20, 2025, wherein the State indicates that it has been diligently turning over discovery, responding to defense questions and concerns regarding discovery, and will continue to do so. The Court conducted a telephone conference with counsel for the State and Defense on November 21, 2025; counsel for both parties waived oral argument and the Court to rule on the Request for Continuance based on the filings.

Where a criminal defendant elects to proceed under Georgia's Criminal Procedure Discovery Act (as Defendant has elected to do in the present case), the State is generally required to produce discovery at least ten days prior to trial. See generally OCGA § 17- 16-4. Having reviewed Defendant's request, and the State's response, the Court finds that the State is in compliance with its discovery obligations. Further, the Court finds that all necessary discovery will likely be provided sufficiently in advance of the January 5, 2026 trial date to afford Defendant a fair trial.

Accordingly, Defendant's Request for Continuance is DENIED.

12-03-2025
Judge Hightower issues an Order Denying Certificate of Immediate Review on December 1, 2025.

Now presented is Defendant's Request for Certificate of Immediate Review, filed December 1, 2025. Defendant seeks review of this Court's Order Denying Motion to Change Venue entered December 1, 2025. See OCGA § 17-7-150(c) ("Notwithstanding other laws, the denial of a motion to change venue shall be appealable immediately only with a certificate of immediate review. Otherwise, the denial shall be appealed with the merits of the case.").

As alluded to in this Court's order denying change of venue, the existing record is insufficient to establish either actual or inherent prejudice at this time. Potential jurors should first be summoned and examined through voir dire to determine whether a panel of impartial jurors, capable of laying aside any impression or opinion of the case and rendering a verdict based on the evidence presented in court, can be drawn from the community. See Chancey v. State, 256 Ga. 415, 431-32 (1986) (outlining standard for qualifying veniremen). See also Id. at 429 (observing that "the empanelling of fair and impartial jurors, as demonstrated on voir dire, makes it particularly difficult to show that the setting of the trial was inherently prejudicial"). Further, this Court noted within the denial order, and reiterates here, that the Court will reconsider a motion to change venue to the extent juror prejudice is shown during the course of the jury selection process. See Hardwick v. State, 264 Ga. 161, 162 FN 2 (1994) (indicating that immediate review is not merited before the trial court entirely disposes of the motion to change venue).

Accordingly, Defendant's Request for Certificate of Immediate Review is hereby DENIED.
