// INCIDENT NARRATIVE — HOW VANTAGE POINT UNFOLDED

On March 4th, Meridian Analytics' SOC flagged an anomalous outbound connection from a finance workstation. What followed was the unraveling of an eight-month infiltration by a sophisticated threat actor the FBI would later designate as APT-VANTAGE.

The breach began with a spear-phishing email sent to a senior analyst, spoofing a trusted vendor. The email carried a weaponized PDF that exploited a zero-day vulnerability in the document reader. Once executed, a custom implant established persistence via a scheduled task—a method that evaded both the endpoint AV and the weekly vulnerability scans.

APT-VANTAGE moved laterally through flat network segments with minimal resistance—no microsegmentation, and service accounts with excessive privileges. They reached the satellite imagery database within 72 hours of initial compromise. Data was exfiltrated in small, encrypted bursts to cloud storage buckets controlled by the actor, staying under the SIEM's volume-alert threshold for months.

Detection finally came from a threat-hunting exercise that correlated anomalous beacon intervals against baselines—not from automated alerting. By then, an estimated 40 GB of classified imagery analysis data had left the network.

The post-breach review found compounding governance failures: an expired third-party risk assessment for the vendor whose identity was spoofed, a missing MFA requirement for remote access, and an incident response plan that hadn't been tested in 22 months. The breach was entirely preventable.
