July 3–6, 2026
Nolan Wells

An 18-year-old wide receiver vanished from a crowded barrier island on July 4, 2026. Two days later, his body washed ashore. This is the timeline of conflicting accounts, unanswered questions, and an ongoing investigation into the circumstances surrounding his death.

This page is under construction while I continue sorting the all-over-the-place media reports and updates.

Evening · July 3
Last Dinner

Nolan made salmon dinner for his parents. Before leaving to spend the night at a friend's house, he hugged his mom and told her he loved her. It was the last time she saw him alive.

Early Morning · July 4
Fourth of July Plans

Warren and Nolan had stayed overnight at a friend's house. Warren ran home then hit McDonald's for breakfast, and met up with Wyatt to top off the boat with gas + load it with ice. Wyatt launched the Palm Beach at the Fort Bayou boat launch and drove it to Bart's grandparents' dock, where the group coordinated before heading out.

Nolan, Warren, and roughly 20 to 30 friends were bound for Horn Island. Approximately two to three thousand people were expected there for the holiday weekend.

11:14 AM · July 4
Horn Island Arrival

The group's boats arrived at the northwestern tip of Horn Island. Three rows of boats stretched for miles along the shoreline. The beach was packed. Jayvon told ABC that he saw Nolan after they arrived, explaining "As soon as we got out there, Nolan's told me that he loved me."

Nolan had called Tracestin twice around 11:00 AM to find where he was anchored. Tracestin missed both calls but spotted their boat pulling in, directed them to an open spot, and helped set the stern anchor.

The boats anchored in formation along the shore: the Tideline was closest to shore, the Palm Beach in the middle, and the Triton on the outer side. They were in the third row out—the point where the water drops sharply from neck-deep to 17–20 feet. The current near the west tip of the island is notably strong.

~4:00 PM · July 4
'He Threw the First Punch'

A stranger interjected himself into an argument between Tracestin and his girlfriend. Tracestin told him twice to step away. The stranger threw the first punch. Nolan was not involved—he was with Katie elsewhere on the island.

~4:15 PM · July 4
Bilge Pump Fails

When Warren checked on the Triton, water was up to the batteries and the motor wouldn't respond so Wyatt threw a rear anchor. Bart, on the Tideline, offered to tow them clear of the crowd. The flooding had built up over hours: heavy foot traffic from people climbing on and off the boat throughout the day gradually dragged water in over the transom, overwhelming the bilge pump.

4:25–4:30 PM · July 4
Nolan Stays Behind

Wyatt called for Nolan multiple times but Nolan told him "I'm staying out here. I'm going to ride in with somebody else." At least 5 to 8 people heard him, according to Warren.

5:52 PM · July 4
The Triton Heads East

The damaged Triton changes course eastward toward the Fort Bayou boat launch. The motor had a trim failure: it would drop into the water but spring back up when put in gear, causing the stop-start GPS pattern. Holding the manual trim override allowed them to maintain speed. Wyatt jumps in to pull the drain plug; the crew shifts weight to the bow to plane out at 40+ mph and expel the water.

~6:00 PM · July 4
Last Known Sighting

Warren personally spoke with someone who saw Nolan on Horn Island around 6:00 PM—approximately 90 minutes after the boats departed. Warren: "We weren't even the last people to see Nolan." Dozens of boats were still anchored around the island at that hour.

6:34 PM · July 4
The Triton Underway

The Triton continues underway near Fort Bayou. GPS coordinates track the vessel's continued movement toward the boat launch, approximately 40 minutes after the boat's previously logged position.

7:19 PM · July 4
Fort Bayou Arrival

The Triton arrives at the Fort Bayou boat launch. This marks the end of the evening return voyage from the island and represents the geographic anchor showing boats back at the mainland launch area.

7:47 PM · July 4
The Triton Trailered to Biloxi

The Triton departs the Fort Bayou boat launch toward Biloxi. GPS tracking shows the route east along the coast, marking the furthest documented waypoint at approximately 22 miles from Fort Bayou, where the damaged vessel is ultimately trailered.

Around Midnight · July 5
Where Are Nolan's Keys?

Nolan's parents head to Warren's house to get his vehicle and search for him. Several friends answer the door. It's tense. One friend who was asleep comes out and says he doesn't know where the keys are. After 15 minutes of searching, they still can't find them.

Nolan's father overhears one of the boys on the phone with a girl, who says the keys are on the XBox. About 5 minutes later, they find them.

~12:30 AM · July 5
Tashema Picks Up Nolan's Phone

Before calling the Coast Guard, Warren had checked Nolan's Snapchat location. Wyatt confirmed the phone was showing as being in his truck. Warren told Christine they had the phone before dialing the Coast Guard.

Around 12:30 AM, an unidentified person calls the group — very aggravated, asks "Where are y'all at?" — then hangs up. About 30 minutes later, Tashema Hands (Jayvon's mother) arrives unannounced with a girl and roughly 8–9 others. She demands Nolan's phone. Wyatt goes to his truck and hands it over. Per Warren: "No complications, no issue." When the phone is later reviewed by the family, Christine finds zero saved photos or videos from the island. Warren: "We had no reason to delete anything."

11:49 PM · July 5
Missing Person Report Filed

Christine calls Ocean Springs Police to report Nolan missing. They tell her to contact the Department of Marine Resources (DMR) because the incident happened on the island, which is federal land.

When she calls DMR, she's told she needs to file with Ocean Springs Police first. Eventually, someone from DMR starts the missing person report to avoid further delay.

Around Midnight · July 5
Meet with DMR Officer

Nolan's parents meet with an officer from DMR at a McDonald's parking lot to provide details for the missing person report. Warren has already contacted the Coast Guard directly to report him missing.

2:53 AM · July 5
Evan posts photo of Nolan

Evan Noel posts a photo on Instagram showing Nolan in the pool with their friends. The image fuels speculation until someone clarifies the pool party occurred on June 27th—not July 4th.

Around this time, the friends' lawyers advise them to stop cooperating with investigators and retain legal representation.

6:00 AM · July 5
Nolan's Coach Joins The Search

Nolan's coach, who saw social media posts about him missing, contacts Nolan's father on Facebook and offers to take him out on his boat to look for Nolan. They head out to Horn Island to search. Nolan's father sees a family in a tent that appears to be living on the island.

~7:00 AM · July 5
Friends Return to Horn Island

Tracestin was up at 7:00 AM the next morning. He, Wyatt, and others took Wyatt's boat back out to search Horn Island.

9:45 AM–Noon · July 5
Nolan's Friends Head Back Out To Search

Tracestin, Wyatt, and other searched Horn Island in Wyatt's boat from 9:45 AM until approximately noon. A rescue official told them professionals had the area under control and directed them to use social media instead.

They returned to shore and spent the rest of Sunday and through the night searching online and sharing everything they could, trying to reach anyone who might have spoken to Nolan after he was last seen.

12:26 PM · July 5
Official Search Launches

The U.S. Coast Guard Sector Mobile receives a request for assistance from Jackson County Sheriff's Office. An urgent marine information broadcast is issued. A helicopter is sent to search the area. Multiple agencies mobilize: Coast Guard, Mississippi Department of Marine Resources (DMR), U.S. National Park Service, Gulf Islands National Seashore.

5:54 PM · July 5
Command Post Established

Jackson County Sheriff's Office establishes a command post at Lake Mars Pier. Sheriff Ledbetter coordinates with multiple agencies.

The United Cajun Navy, a nonprofit volunteer search and rescue group from Louisiana, joins the effort. Their vice president tells CNN: if someone fell in the water, strong rip currents that day would have posed a serious risk.

5:00–6:00 AM · July 6
Tracestin Preps to Search

Tracestin was up at 5:00 AM. He fueled his boat at 6:00 AM with plans to take the boat back out to the Chandelier Islands to expand the search area. He put his boat on the lift and stopped at McDonald's first.

6:00 AM · July 6
UCN Continues Search

The United Cajun Navy continues searching via land, air, and sea. Search boats are deployed and the last vessels leave just before 8:00 AM to continue the search for Nolan.

After 8:40 AM · July 6
News Reaches Tracestin

While leaving McDonald's, Tracestin got the news: Nolan's body had been found.

"I wasn't there to help him in his last minutes," he said. "But now I do everything in my power to help." He donated to the family, reached out personally, and has since given multiple on-record interviews to clear up what he says are false narratives about his friend group.

8:45 AM+ · July 6
Family Notified

Sheriff Ledbetter confirms authorities received a call reporting a body found at the northwestern tip of Horn Island. Nolan's family is asked to come to Lake Mars Pier, where they meet with Sheriff Ledbetter and Coroner Bruce Lynd. They are told there are no immediate, obvious signs of foul play or trauma on the body.

11:00 AM · July 6
Identification Confirmed

The family is taken to the Coroner's Office in Pascagoula. The body is confirmed via dental records. It is Nolan Wells.

1:00 PM · July 6
Public Confirmation

Nolan's family confirms his death on social media. Christine posts on Facebook: the body found on Horn Island was their son. The Coroner's Office requests the autopsy be conducted at the state medical examiner's office due to the "condition" of the body and to determine if there was any trauma or foul play.

July 7, 2026
Legal Team Retained

The Wells family retains nationally renowned civil rights attorney Ben Crump who immediately calls for a "thorough and transparent investigation." Crump tells ABC News the family is concerned about South Mississippi law enforcement investigating a case where a "young Black man" may have been involved with "young white students who may have some culpability."

July 7, 2026
Independent Autopsy Ordered

The family's legal team orders an independent autopsy. Nolan's body is flown to Washington, D.C., for examination by experienced forensic pathologist Dr. Roger Mitchell. The state medical examiner conducts an autopsy in Mississippi. Cause of death: pending investigation. Date of death: unknown pending investigation.

July 22, 2026
Autopsy Findings

At the NAACP National Convention in Chicago, Ben Crump announces preliminary findings from the independent autopsy (Dr. Roger Mitchell, Washington D.C.). Cause and manner of death: undetermined, pending investigation. Marked decompositional changes; no fractures or deep tissue injuries. A 12-by-8-inch area of red discoloration in soft tissue on the back of Nolan's head. The throat was not received with the body. Toxicology results not yet available.

Crump: "The condition of Nolan Wells before he entered the water would be the crux of the matter—whether he was conscious or unconscious, forced or unforced. It would be of utmost importance to determining cause and manner of death."

Christine: "The only question we have is, what happened to our son?"

July 23, 2026 · Present
Investigation Continues

Over 60 witnesses have been interviewed. The FBI is assisting with digital evidence. District Attoney McIlrath confirmed the state medical examiner's autopsy report will be withheld until a grand jury reviews it. Rewards totaling $125,000 have been offered.

Critical questions remain unanswered: What were the circumstances that led to Nolan entering the water? Was there water in the airway? What are the toxicology findings? Investigators await answers.
