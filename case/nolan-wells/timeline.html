July 3–6, 2026
Nolan Wells

An 18-year-old wide receiver vanished from a crowded barrier island on July 4, 2026. Two days later, his body washed ashore. This is the timeline of conflicting accounts, unanswered questions, and an ongoing investigation into the circumstances surrounding his death.

This page is under construction while I continue sorting the all-over-the-place media reports and updates.

Evening · July 3
Last Dinner

Nolan made salmon dinner for his parents. Before leaving to spend the night at Warren's house, he hugged his mom. "Hey mom, I'm about to go. I love you," he said. She hugged him back. It was the last time she saw him alive.

Early Morning · July 4
Fourth of July Plans

Warren woke at a buddy's house, ran to McDonald's, then met up with Wyatt to top off the boat with gas and load it with ice. The group coordinated at Bart's grandparents' house before heading out. Nolan, Warren, and roughly 20–30 friends were bound for Horn Island, a federally protected barrier island known for its untouched beaches and isolation. Approximately 2,000–3,000+ people were expected there for the holiday weekend.

9:56 AM · July 4
Boats Depart

Three boats left from a private dock near El Camino Real Rd., carrying ~20 to 30 people total. Nolan rode on Wyatt's 22 Triton (8–9 people). Warren rode on Bart's 24 Tide Line (10–16 people). Wyatt's 18 Palm Beach carried 4 guys. Jayvon Williams, a close friend, was going to ride with Nolan but that boat filled up, so he went on a different vessel. Tracestin Shepherd, another friend who was part of the group, rode separately on his uncle's boat, which carried 14 people in total.

11:14 AM · July 4
Horn Island Arrival

The group's boats arrived at the northwestern tip of Horn Island. Three rows of boats stretched for miles along the shoreline. The beach was packed. Jayvon saw Nolan after they arrived. He told ABC News: "As soon as we got out there, Nolan told me that he loved me." Nolan had called Tracestin twice around 11:00 AM to find where he was anchored. Tracestin missed both calls but spotted their boat pulling in, directed them to an open spot, and helped set the stern anchor.

~2:00 PM · July 4
Meeting Katie

Nolan met a girl named Katie. They spent the afternoon together on the beach. Around 3 PM, Warren caught his last glimpse of Nolan: "Nolan was cradling Katie in his arms—shaking his head, laughing. I was like, 'Dude, you crazy.'"

~3:50 PM · July 4
"All right, buddy. I love you. See you later."

About 10 minutes before the fight, Tracestin and his girlfriend were walking back toward his uncle's boat. Nolan crossed their path going the opposite direction—toward Wyatt's boat. They dabbed up. Tracestin made a joking comment about the girl Nolan was with. His last words to Nolan: "All right, buddy. I love you. See you later."

"I'm glad I said it," Tracestin told interviewers in July 2026. "That was my last encounter with Nolan. Was telling him I love him."

~4:00 PM · July 4
Altercation on Island

A video circulated online showing someone yelling: "Give me my f-ing phone back." Many initially believed it was Nolan. Later, Tracestin Shepherd clarified: "That's me yelling." Jayvon confirmed: "We were calming Tracestin down in that video."

According to Tracestin, the fight had nothing to do with Nolan. A stranger interjected himself into an argument between Tracestin and his girlfriend over a dropped koozie lid. Tracestin told him twice to step away. The stranger threw the first punch. Tracestin's uncle pulled him onto the boat. Carter — Warren's brother and a close friend of Tracestin's little brother — then confronted the stranger verbally: "If you got a problem with Tracestin, we can handle it right here." Tracestin says he was the only Black person involved in the entire altercation; the stranger was white, Carter was white. Tracestin left the island 30–45 minutes after the fight. Ben Crump later claimed the person in the fight was Nolan. Tracestin's response: "Nice try — but there was another Black guy on the island."

Katie reportedly said she last saw Nolan around this time, still with his friends.

~4:00 PM · July 4
He Threw the First Punch

The fight started when a stranger, unknown to Tracestin and not part of the group, walked up and interjected himself into a private argument between Tracestin and his girlfriend. She was upset about a koozie lid he had dropped in the water. Tracestin told the man twice to step away. The stranger threw the first punch.

Tracestin's uncle pulled him onto the boat. Carter, Warren's younger brother who is close friends with Tracestin's little brother, then confronted the man verbally: "If you got a problem with Tracestin, we can handle it right here." Tracestin says he was the only black person involved in the entire altercation; the stranger was white and Carter was white. Nolan was not involved.

Ben Crump later claimed the person yelling in the video was Nolan. Tracestin: "Nice try but there was another Black guy on the island. Now your whole narrative needs to change again."

~4:15 PM · July 4
Bilge Pump Fails

Warren checked on the Triton. Water was up to the batteries; the motor wouldn't respond. Wyatt threw a rear anchor. Bart, on the Tide Line, offered to tow them clear of the crowd.

4:25–4:30 PM · July 4
Nolan Stays Behind

Wyatt and crew shouted for people to get off or get on the tow. They called for Nolan multiple times: "Nolan, get on the boat! We're leaving — the boat's having issues!" Nolan responded: "No, I'm staying out here. I'm going to ride in with somebody else." At least 5–8 people heard him refuse. Katie later texted a friend: "The last time I saw him was when they were yelling for him to get on the boat." The Palm Beach had already left. Multiple people in the group routinely rode out on one boat and back on another — Warren described it as not uncommon.

4:31 PM · July 4
Triton in Distress

The Triton reports a distress situation. Water is still entering the vessel. Emergency response is initiated. GPS coordinates mark the boat's position between the island and mainland as it heads toward the coast for emergency assistance.

The Palm Beach Returns

The Palm Beach boat returns to the El Camino Real Rd. dock. GPS tracking documents the return of one of the three boats that departed earlier that morning.

5:52 PM · July 4
The Triton Heads East

The damaged Triton, towed by Bart's Tide Line, changes course eastward toward the Fort Bayou boat launch. Once there, Wyatt and crew jump in the water to reinstall the drain plug, then plane the boat off at 40+ mph to expel the excess water.

~6:00 PM · July 4
Last Known Sighting

Someone close to the group reports seeing Nolan at around 6:30 PM on Horn Island, approximately two hours after the group's boats departed. There were still dozens of boats anchored around the island.

6:34 PM · July 4
The Triton Underway

The Triton continues underway near Fort Bayou. GPS coordinates track the vessel's continued movement toward the boat launch, two minutes after the previous position fix.

7:19 PM · July 4
Fort Bayou Arrival

The Triton arrives at the Fort Bayou location. This marks the end of the evening return voyage from the island and represents the geographic anchor showing boats back at the mainland launch area.

7:47 PM · July 4
The Triton Trailered to Biloxi

The Triton departs Fort Bayou toward Biloxi. GPS tracking shows the route east along the coast, marking the furthest documented waypoint at approximately 22 miles from Fort Bayou, where the damaged vessel is ultimately trailered.

~9:20 PM · July 4
Warren calls Tracetin

Warren called Tracestin at approximately 9:20 PM. Tracestin was asleep on his uncle's boat and didn't answer.

~11:00 PM · July 4
"They're Looking for Nolan"

Tracestin's brother woke him a little after 11:00 PM: "Warren's trying to get a hold of you. They're looking for Nolan." Tracestin called back on a mutual friend's phone. Warren was on speakerphone with the other guys in the room.

At that point Warren was already dialing the Coast Guard. Tracestin's initial read: "He's probably just out somewhere with that girl. Get a hold of the girl—you'll probably find Nolan." Warren: "We're starting to freak out." Tracestin: "Okay." Warren hung up to dial Coast Guard. That was their last contact until the next morning.

~11:15 PM · July 4
Friends Call Nolan's Parents

One of Nolan's friends called his mother to say Nolan still hadn't returned from the island. His mother called Tashema Hands, mother of Jayvon Williams, to ask if she'd seen him. Jayvon and his brother check Nolan's Snapchat location. His mother checks Life360. The data doesn't match.

Around Midnight · July 5
Phone Handed Over

Tashema Hands, mother of Jayvon, arrives at a home of one of the boys' friends to get Nolan's phone. She asks for his phone. Warren gave an interview stating the phone was left on the boat and placed in Wyatt's truck, and that they handed it over immediately with no resistance. Nolan's mother gave an interview stating that when she received his phone and began looking through it for any information that could explain where he was, she was perplexed to find zero saved photos or videos from the boat or the island. Warren has since stated they had no reason to delete anything from it.

Around Midnight · July 5
Where Are the Keys?

Nolan's parents arrive at Warren's house to get his vehicle and search for him. Several friends answer the door. It's tense. One friend who was asleep comes out and says he doesn't know where the keys are. After 15 minutes of searching, they still can't find them. Nolan's father overhears one of the boys on the phone with a girl, who says the keys are on the XBox. About 5 minutes later, they find them.

Around Midnight · July 5
Missing Person Report Filed

Nolan's mother calls Ocean Springs Police to report him missing. They tell her to contact the Department of Marine Resources (DMR) because the incident happened on the island, which is federal land. When she calls DMR, she's told she needs to file with Ocean Springs Police first. Eventually, someone from DMR starts the missing person report to avoid further delay.

Around Midnight · July 5
Meet with DMR Officer

Nolan's parents meet with an officer from DMR at a McDonald's parking lot to provide details for the missing person report. One of Nolan's friends also contacts the Coast Guard directly to report him missing.

2:53 AM · July 5
Photo Surfaces on Instagram

Evan Noel posts a photo on Instagram showing Nolan in a pool. The image fuels speculation until someone clarifies the pool party occurred on June 27th, not July 4th.

Around this time, the friends' lawyers advise them to stop cooperating with investigators and retain legal representation.

6:00 AM · July 5
Private Search Begins

Nolan's coach, who saw social media posts about him missing, contacts Nolan's father on Facebook and offers to take him out on his boat. They go to Horn Island to search. Nolan's father sees a family in a tent that appears to be living on the island.

~7:00 AM · July 5
Friends Return to Horn Island

Tracestin was up at 7 AM on Sunday, July 5. He, Wyatt, and others took Wyatt's boat, still in the water, back out to search Horn Island.

9:45 AM–Noon · July 5
Back on the Water

Tracestin, Wyatt, and other searched Horn Island in Wyatt's boat from 9:45 AM to approximately noon. A rescue official told them professionals had the area under control and directed them to use social media instead.

They returned to shore and spent the rest of Sunday and through the night searching online and sharing everything they could, trying to reach anyone who might have spoken to Nolan after he was last seen.

12:26 PM · July 5
Official Search Launches

The U.S. Coast Guard Sector Mobile receives a request for assistance from Jackson County Sheriff's Office. An urgent marine information broadcast is issued. A helicopter is sent to search the area. Multiple agencies mobilize: Coast Guard, Mississippi Department of Marine Resources, U.S. National Park Service, Gulf Islands National Seashore.

5:54 PM · July 5
Command Post Established

Jackson County Sheriff's Office establishes a command post at Lake Mars Boat Launch. Sheriff John Ledbetter coordinates with multiple agencies. The United Cajun Navy, a nonprofit volunteer search and rescue group from Louisiana, joins the effort. Their vice president tells CNN: if someone fell in the water, strong rip currents that day would have posed a serious risk.

~5:00–6:00 AM · July 6
Tracetin Got the News at McDonald's

Tracestin was up at 5:00 AM on Monday, July 6. He fueled his boat at 6 AM. The plan was to take another boat out to the Chandelier Islands at 7 AM to expand the search area. He put his boat on the lift and stopped at McDonald's first. While leaving McDonald's, he got the news: Nolan's body had been found.

"I wasn't there to help him in his last minutes," he said. "But now I do everything in my power to help." He donated to the family, reached out personally, and has since given multiple on-record interviews to clear up what he says are false narratives about his friend group.

6:00 AM · July 6
UCN Continues Search

The United Cajun Navy begins searching via land, air, and sea. Search boats are deployed at 8:00 AM. The last vessels leave just before 8 AM to continue the search for Nolan.

8:40 AM · July 6
Body Found

A U.S. Park Service ranger discovers a body face down in the water at the western tip of Horn Island where the boats had been anchored. The body matches Nolan's description: 6'1", approximately 180 pounds, wearing blue swim trunks.

8:45 AM+ · July 6
Family Notified

Sheriff Ledbetter confirms authorities received a call reporting a body found at the northwestern tip of Horn Island. Nolan's family is asked to come to Lake Mars Boat Launch, where they meet with Sheriff Ledbetter and Coroner Bruce Lynd. There are no immediate, obvious signs of foul play or trauma on the body.

11:00 AM · July 6
Identification Confirmed

The family is taken to the Coroner's Office in Pascagoula. The body is confirmed via dental records. It is Nolan Wells.

1:00 PM · July 6
Public Confirmation

Nolan's family confirms his death on social media. His mother posts on Facebook: the body found on Horn Island was their son. The coroner's office requests the autopsy be conducted at the state medical examiner's office due to the "condition" of the body and to determine if there was any trauma or foul play.

July 7, 2026
Legal Team Retained

The Wells family retains nationally renowned civil rights attorney Ben Crump. Crump calls for a "thorough and transparent investigation." Crump tells ABC News: the family is concerned about South Mississippi law enforcement investigating a case where a "young Black man" may have been involved with "young white students who may have some culpability."

July 7, 2026
Independent Autopsy Ordered

The family's legal team orders an independent autopsy. Nolan's body is flown to Washington, D.C., for examination by experienced forensic pathologist Dr. Roger Mitchell. The state medical examiner conducts an autopsy in Mississippi. Cause of death: pending investigation. Date of death: unknown pending investigation.

July 22, 2026
Autopsy Findings

At the NAACP National Convention, Ben Crump announces preliminary findings from the independent autopsy. Cause and manner of death: undetermined "pending investigation." The second autopsy revealed: no lacerations of skin or fractures of skull, but a 12-by-8-inch area of red discoloration in soft tissue on the back of Nolan's head. The throat was not received with the body, making it impossible to rule out injury to the neck as a cause of death.

July 23, 2026 · Present
Investigation Continues

Over 60 witnesses have been interviewed. The FBI is assisting with digital evidence. District Attorney Angel Myers McIlrath confirmed the state medical examiner's autopsy report will be withheld until a grand jury reviews it. Rewards totaling $125,000 have been offered. Critical questions remain unanswered: What were the circumstances that led to Nolan entering the water? Was there water in the airway? What are the toxicology findings? Investigators await answers.
