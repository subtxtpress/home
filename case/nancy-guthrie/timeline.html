2:28 AM: Roughly 40 minutes after the doorbell camera went offline, Nancy’s pacemaker lost its remote monitoring connection. Officials have not confirmed the exact mechanism.

The pacemaker has no GPS capability and cannot be tracked remotely. It will continue functioning on its own indefinitely, and is not designed to keep her heart beating and "not to function like a live health tracker or GPS device," per cardiologist Dr. Antony Chu.

If the device is ever recovered, investigators can directly interrogate it to retrieve time-stamped cardiac data. If she does not survive, the exact moment her heart stopped will be on record.

Forbes

“Pacemakers are really designed to remotely capture data and then push it to the cloud,” Chu explained. The information sent is limited, technical and meant for doctors to review later, not for real-time viewing. Patient apps often show little more than whether a transmission occurred. Tuesday, Feb. 17, 2026
DNA Results Negative; Genealogical Search Planned
Day 17

Sheriff Chris Nanos announced that DNA recovered from a glove found two miles from Nancy Guthrie's home does not match DNA found inside her home. Neither DNA sample produced a hit in the FBI's Combined DNA Index System (CODIS).

With no matches in law enforcement databases, the PCSD is now turning to commercial genealogical databases like Ancestry.com to search for familial matches to the glove DNA. This method, made famous by the 2018 arrest of Golden State Killer Joseph James DeAngelo, involves building family trees from relative DNA uploads to identify suspects.
