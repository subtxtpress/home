Who Took Holly Bobo?

On the morning of April 13, 2011, a 20-year-old nursing student was taken from her home in rural Decatur County, Tennessee, and was never seen alive again.

This is the geographic story of that morning, told through cell tower pings, phone records, testimony, and physical evidence introduced at the trial of Zachary Adams.

(This is a living document being updated as I round up and double check details.)

Zach's Apr. 4th Natchez Trace arrest

Apr. 4, 2011 traffic stop: A state park officer pulled Zach over after he drove the wrong way down a road at high speed past cars already stopped for downed power lines.

During the stop, the officer found a camo fanny pack in the car. Zach grabbed the bag and ran into the woods, throwing items as he fled. The officer caught him, pushed him to the ground, and arrested him, during which Zach attempted to scratch the officer with his fingernails. Zach was alleged to have attempted to run over officers with his truck.

His truck was impounded by the Henderson County Sheriff's Department and later retrieved by his grandfather, Dick Adams.

Bail bondsman Kristie Gutgsell bailed him out. At the time of bail, Gutgsell observed scratches all over him and what she described as "old scars" on his arms.

The officer testified at trial that, at the time of the Apr. 4th arrest, Zach did not have long, linear scratches on his left arm. No injury report was filed for Zach.

Rebecca Earp, Zach's girlfriend, testified at trial that the scratches came from the state park officer chase. She also placed the timing. She confirmed Zach had never made any comments about Holly before her disappearance.

The two observations—no scratches on Apr. 4th, and scratches visible at the Natchez Trace bail—bracket the abduction date.

Shayne's Apr. 12th whereabouts & appearance

Night before the abduction: Shayne spent the evening drinking and playing cards.

His parents drove him (as was their regular routine) because Shayne had no working transportation after he totaled his truck. His mother Rita took his keys away and later produced them to an FBI agent.

Family relationships: Holly Bobo, Jason Autry, and Shayne Austin are all related. Some of the Austin family visited Jason Autry in prison regularly.

Autry's trial account—what he testified to happening at the I-840 bridge (and later recanted)

According to Autry's trial testimony, after Zach told him he needed help "burying a body," Autry agreed under the condition that his PT Cruiser be parked somewhere other than Shayne's. He left it at a church and got into Zach's white Nissan Frontier.

He asked Zach about shovels; Zach had none. The plan, Autry testified, was to gut Holly and put her in the "slew" under a bridge so turtles would eat her—an idea Autry said came from having once seen a body floating under a bridge held up by its intestines. He denied on cross-examination suggesting the gutting plan himself.

They drove toward the I-840 bridge on the Tennessee River. Zach backed the truck up to a pile of riprap—loose limestone off to the side of the bridge—20–30 feet from the water. Autry grabbed the upper torso of the body with his right hand; Zach lowered the tailgate and grabbed her legs. Autry set her head on the riprap. He described a small amount of blood in the truck bed and an orange-sized bloodspot on the multicolored farm-style blanket. He said he never unwrapped the blanket or looked inside.

Then, Autry testified, he saw what he believed was a foot move and heard a sound of distress. He told Zach: "This fucking bitch is still alive." He added that she had heard his name, though on cross-examination he could not say when his name had been said—or confirm she could have heard anything from inside a wrapped blanket in the back of a truck.

Zach, who had been digging in a fanny pack, turned around and retrieved a pistol from the floorboards of the truck—the same gun, Autry testified, that Shayne had holstered on his right side at the trailer that morning. Autry said he told Zach to wait until he confirmed no one was watching, ran to check the area, came back, and then heard the gun fire. He described the sound as "boom"—three or four shots he then attributed to an echo. A boat was then heard on the river. Autry said he ran back to the truck, told Zach they had been seen, and they quickly reloaded the body—the bloodspot, he now said, had grown to the size of a grapefruit. They put Holly back in the truck, shut the tailgate, and left.

On the ride back, Autry says Zach explained the motive: Natalie, a woman Zach was involved with, was working at a strip club for drugs, had told Zach that Holly had been to his house, and had "left him with the idea" that the two of them would have a threesome with Holly. Zach had shown Autry pictures of Holly that Natalie had provided.

Cross-examination inconsistencies: Autry testified on direct that he did not notice the gun on the truck's floorboard when he got in. On cross, he said he did notice it. He said they originally agreed to meet in "the thicket," but at one point referred to it as the Marathon Station. He testified he did not suggest gutting Holly but had described the plan in detail on direct. When Thompson asked why he would warn Zach that Holly had heard his name when she was presumably in the back of a closed truck wrapped in a blanket, he had no answer. In last fall's site visit, Autry was taken from Nashville State Prison to the actual bridge location by lawyers, law enforcement, and prosecutors to demonstrate what he claimed happened.

Apr. 15, 2011: Marathon Station meeting

  • According to Autry's trial testimony (later recanted), Zach called him and they agreed to meet near a Marathon Station about a mile from Zach's home.
  • Dylan was with Zach. They drove a quarter mile into a wooded area behind the station. Zach got into Autry's car alone, leaving Dylan in the other vehicle.
  • He asked Autry what it would take to kill Dylan—who, he said, "kept talking and wouldn't sleep."
  • Zach's offer: Dick Adams was expected to pass away soon, and if Jason killed Dylan, Zach would give him a share of the inheritance.
  • Autry said he would think about it. He later told Zach that if he killed Dylan, Zach would be next. Zach also told Autry he had dumped Holly's body near Kennedy Bridge.
  • Shortly after, Autry drove to Shayne's trailer, stopping at Dottie's to buy beer.
  • He ran into a roadblock staffed by Jeremy Inman, who searched his vehicle.

At Shayne's: Autry found him "highly inebriated" and "frantic."

  • A command post had been set up at the nearby church.
  • Autry put Shayne in his car, took him to Johnny's Bar and Grill to eat, then to another bar, and eventually dropped him back at the trailer before returning to Angela's house.

Apr. 16–17, 2011: Autry at Kelly's Ridge

  • The weekend after Holly's disappearance, Brenda O'Bryant was at her parents' home, which sits directly across from Kelly's Ridge—a gated dirt road through the woods where people regularly hunt.
  • A silver PT Cruiser pulled into the driveway. The driver asked if he could fish in the ponds on the property.
  • Her mother told him there weren't any fish, due to river otters. He asked where her father was.
  • When she told him her father was out looking for Holly, the man—who she later identified as Jason Autry—became visibly "stressed" and said he would return later.
  • Brenda reported this to law enforcement a few days later and followed up with a written email. She acknowledged that Kelly Ridge Road had been gated off for years.

Apr. 24, 2011: THP Officer Warren Rainey visits Zach Adams

  • About a week and a half after the abduction, Tennessee Highway Patrol Officer Warren Rainey—working as a searcher, not on direct instruction from law enforcement—goes to Zach Adams' residence because "his name kept coming up."
  • He arrives visibly armed and in uniform.
  • Zach appears to have just woken up. He is visibly nervous and shaking, and repeatedly denies involvement.
  • When Rainey follows Zach inside as he goes to put on a shirt, Zach wants him out of the house.

At trial, defense attorney Jennifer Thompson pressed Rainey on this:

  • "And you'd recognize you have absolutely no legal right to be in his house?"
  • Rainey responded: "I wasn't going to go all the way through his house, but I was not going to let a man that I did not know step in a house and obtain a firearm and shoot me."
  • Rainey acknowledged he had no instruction to speak to Adams and admitted it was plausible Zach was nervous due to having drugs in the home.

After Rainey and his fellow trooper leave, they watch in their mirrors as Zach runs back to his residence and hides up the road. Dylan Adams quickly exits the driveway in a pickup truck, driving so recklessly along back roads that Rainey says there was probable cause to stop him before he reached Parsons. Rainey calls the command post requesting backup, stating he is "very suspicious about Zach Adams." Sheriff Wyatt and a man in a suit later arrive to speak to Zach.

Likely the next day, Rainey assembles a search team with three K-9 units to search hundreds of acres of county property behind the Adams residence. A "decent," not old, mattress is found leaning against the northwest side of the house. A felled tree lays over a roughly 6×8-foot patch of bare dirt with no grass—suspected to be a grave. Investigators dig. Zach retrieves a shovel when asked. It is not a grave. Hundreds of marijuana plants are found growing on the county property. No evidence related to Holly's disappearance is found on the Adams property or in Dylan's truck.

2012 — Cell tower analysis

2012: US Marshal John Walker, SA Terry Dicus, and others developed a map of Holly's cell tower pings tracing her phone's movement on the day of her abduction. Walker had been requested by TBI for his expertise in interpreting cell tower records; he is not authorized to disclose the specific tracking methods used by the US Marshals Service. The map was displayed and explained in court.

Defense cell phone expert summary findings (presented at trial):

  • Zach's phone never overlapped with Holly's based on predictive server mapping (data unavailable at time of abduction, but now able to significantly narrow location ranges).
  • On two separate occasions, Holly and Zach were clearly on different cell sectors at similar times.
  • No correlations between Jason Autry's phone and Holly's phone that day.
  • No records of Jason Autry's phone using Shayne Austin's home tower.
  • Shayne Austin's phone is on his home tower from 9:23 AM through 10:55 AM, with no earlier activity that morning.
  • Zach and Jason's phones do hit the same tower (Birdsong northeast sector) around the same time—consistent with them being together at that location.

At trial, defense private investigator Amber Treat drove three routes in Decatur County to measure time and distance between key locations:

  • Route 1 (Yellow Springs Church → I-40 bridge, Tennessee River): ~26 minutes
  • Route 2 (I-40 bridge → Yellow Springs Church, alternate path): ~32 minutes
  • Route 3 (Holly's phone route per Walker's cell tower interpretation): ~43 minutes
Autry's credibility — cross-examination findings & recantation
  • Autry has been to prison three times for theft and drugs. He was charged in this case at the time of his testimony and testified hoping for leniency.
  • He is represented by Robert Parish and Michael Scholl, who replaced two former attorneys who lost their law licenses.
  • He admits he lied to law enforcement repeatedly, including in media interviews after his arrest, and continued writing letters proclaiming his innocence after learning his mail was being scanned by prison officials. His former attorneys, he implied, had advised him to proclaim innocence.
  • He wrote letters to Lisa Autry and Linda stating he would be out of prison by Christmas and "getting out soon," respectively, while simultaneously claiming to have no knowledge of his sentence.
  • He admits he was in solitary confinement while in prison.
  • He admitted TBI agents "almost broke two of his fingers" during questioning on this case.
  • He confirmed that his attorney Jeff Jackson told him both Zach Adams and Shayne Austin had signed statements against him—suggesting he understood the evidentiary landscape when he decided to cooperate.

The Cox Road gun problem (August 2015 – March 2017):

On August 18, 2015, a Decatur County highway crew mowing the shoulder of Cox Road finds a pistol—a CAR 9 millimeter, serial number IQ7507. The dispatcher who logs it into county records is Sanders—the same dispatcher who took Karen Bobo's 911 call on April 13, 2011.

The timeline problem: The activity date (when the gun was found) is August 18, 2015. The report date (when a TBI agent actually wrote it up) is March 23, 2017—a 19-month gap. A loaded pistol turns up on the shoulder of the exact road the state's whole case runs down and sits unwritten for nearly two years until the exact spring the state suddenly badly needs a gun. On March 7, 2017, Special Agent Brent Booth removes the Cox Road gun from Decatur County Sheriff's Department's evidence and moves it into the TBI vault.

Victor Dinsmore and the changing caliber story (Spring 2017): During this same spring of 2017—while the state is scrambling for evidence—Special Agent Brent Booth is flying witness Victor Dinsmore in and out of Tennessee, digging up creek banks and roadsides for a different gun on a story that keeps changing: first a .38, then a .357, then a .38 with an interchangeable .357 chamber, until finally landing on a corroded .32 revolver after two searches end up empty. Booth told Dinsmore on tape: "This is what's going to be testified to." That .32 is the gun the jury sees.

The 911 recordings problem (March 2017): The state requests the 911 audio from the morning Holly was taken (TBI IR 3120). On March 22, 2017, Decatur County E911 dispatcher Joey Alexander releases the backup copies of the 911 recordings to the TBI. Alexander provides two DVDs dated March 26, 2011 and April 29, 2011 through June 4, 2011. The report is titled for April 13 (the day Holly was taken), but:

  • One disc is marked March 26, 2011—18 days before Holly was taken, but the date of Brennan Duke's death (Holly's inner circle's close friend)
  • The other runs April 29, 2011 through June 4, 2011—skipping the critical window of April 13
  • April 13 is on neither disc
  • The TBI report notes that these DVDs only play on a system Decatur County no longer has, requiring a special system to be located in order to access the recordings
  • Alexander's own phone was one call from the grave phone tower that morning—he was positioned to connect the dispatcher web directly to Derrick Hall's phone at tower 249

Analysis: The dispatcher who answered Holly's 911 call (Sanders) is the same dispatcher who logged the Cox Road gun. The dispatcher who sat one call from the grave phone that morning (Alexander) is the same one who hands over 911 recordings that skipped the day Holly was taken. The agent who moves the Cox Road gun into a vault (Booth) is the same agent pressing Dinsmore to come up with a matching gun and caught on his own recording telling the witness what to remember. Four documents, three names, same pattern.

The Kahr gun's fate: After being logged by Sanders in 2015, moved by Booth into the TBI vault in 2017, and presented as potential evidence while the state searches for a gun that matches testimony, the Kahr 9mm never enters trial. Once another gun is pulled from a creek—the corroded .32 revolver that Booth coached Dinsmore to testify about—the Kahr quietly disappears from the narrative. Why does a loaded pistol found on the exact road the state's case runs down never make it into trial? The answer lies in what happened during those 19 months of silence—and who was standing next to it when it was found.

Brent Booth's reward: In January 2018—just months after moving the controversial Cox Road gun into the TBI vault and coaching Victor Dinsmore on his testimony—Special Agent Brent Booth received the TBI Agent of the Year award. The timing: one month after the 2017 evidence moves and approximately seven months before Zach Adams' trial conviction in August 2018.

Amber Bray's charge disappears: On April 29, 2014—the same day the state first charged Jason Autry in the Bobo case—Amber Bray's kidnapping and assault charge against Zach Adams quietly went away without explanation. She had been charged after claiming Zach held a gun to her head on February 6, but didn't report it until February 14. In between, she visited her sister Trisha's house, found Zach napping, and hit him with a laundry basket. Zach and Trisha called police on her for the altercation. The charge was leverage, and once it served its purpose (getting Zach arrested on the kidnapping charge that justified the search warrant), it vanished from the record.
