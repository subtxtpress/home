Paid Training-to-Hire Programs

Defense Shipbuilding & Manufacturing, 10-2026

The closest matches to ATDM are the shipyard-run "earn while you learn" apprenticeships at Newport News Shipbuilding, Bath Iron Works, Electric Boat, Ingalls and the four Navy public shipyards. These pay a real wage from day one, which is better than a stipend. For short, ATDM-style bootcamps, the best options are Navy-funded regional pipelines: Eastern Connecticut's Manufacturing Pipeline Initiative, BIW's $500/week community college course, Hanwha Philly's stipended welder programs, Hampton Roads' maritime training system, and Michigan's M3. Most of these are regional and require you to live locally. ATDM is still the only one that is nationally recruited and housing-covered.

TL;DR

  • Best overall pick if you can relocate: apply directly to a shipyard apprenticeship or paid-trainee job. Pay runs from about $24/hr (Newport News Apprentice School, $50,856 starting) to $26–31/hr (Portsmouth Naval Shipyard) and $28–30/hr (NNS welder trainee). Tuition is free, and there's a registered DOL credential and/or associate degree at the end. Many 2026 windows have closed (Norfolk on 05-26, Portsmouth on 07-01, BIW on 05-31). Plan for spring 2027 intakes, and apply now to rolling programs like EB paid training, NNS trainee roles and Ingalls.
  • Closest ATDM-style options (short, free, with cash or attendance stipends plus a hiring pipeline): Eastern CT Manufacturing Pipeline Initiative (Electric Boat), BIW/CMCC Manufacturing Technician course ($500/week), Hanwha Philly Welder Training/Welder B ($150–$300/week), and SENEDIA's RI Precision Manufacturing Boot Camp. All are regional, open to civilians and run on rolling or cohort intakes.
  • National entry points: ATDM itself (Danville, VA; tuition, books and housing covered; next Discovery Day is 11-11-2026), BuildSubmarines.com and the new DoD BuildFreedom.US portal. In House Appropriations testimony on 09-04-2026, Assistant Secretary of War Michael Cadenazzi said Build Freedom "aligns more than 70 Industrial Base Analysis & Sustainment (IBAS) workforce projects under one campaign." Of the Manufacturing USA institutes, only LIFT's Operation Next has a real training program. It's free but unpaid, and limited to certain states or military-affiliated people. America Makes has no direct training-to-hire program for individuals.

Key Findings

  1. Shipyard registered apprenticeships are better than a stipend. At these programs you are a full-time employee from day one, with wages, benefits and free tuition. The trade-off is competitive admission, multi-year commitments and fixed annual windows.
  2. Short-term "ATDM-like" programs mostly cover tuition plus an attendance stipend, not a living wage. The cash ranges from performance stipends (Eastern CT MPI) to $150–$500 a week (Hanwha Philly, BIW/CMCC). ATDM is unusual in covering housing so out-of-state people can attend.
  3. ATDM covers different trades than you might expect. It teaches additive manufacturing, CNC machining, non-destructive testing, quality control/metrology and welding. It does not teach pipefitting or shipfitting. If you want those trades specifically, the shipyard programs (NNS, EB, BIW, Ingalls, the Navy yards) are the right route.
  4. Several Navy-funded pilots are ending or between cohorts. Macomb's M3 says its final Navy-funded cohort starts 10-26-2026. Hampton Roads' "Pathways to Shipbuilding" was a single-year 2025 grant. Confirm a program is still running before planning around it.
  5. "NSRR" isn't a separate program. The Navy's workforce push now runs through the Maritime Industrial Base (MIB) Program and its Submarine Industrial Base (SIB) Program Office. They fund ATDM, BuildSubmarines.com, the BlueForge Alliance Talent Pipeline Program, M3, and regional projects in Hampton Roads, Connecticut, Rhode Island, Alabama and elsewhere. The size of the gap: the MIB Program Office's June 2025 MIB-101 brief says the program "requires ~250K skilled maritime trades personnel over next decade." The submarine-only figure often cited is 140,000. GAO reported in 04-2026 that DoD "has invested more than $10 billion since fiscal year 2018" in the submarine industrial base. The Navy's own SIB FAQ gives a higher figure of more than $15 billion.

E Manufacturing USA institutes and DoD portals

  • LIFT: Operation Next (DoD Manufacturing USA institute, Detroit): free, hybrid training in welding, CNC, industrial maintenance and robotics, with credentials. It is a DoD SkillBridge program. Civilians are currently eligible only in Michigan and Montana; Indiana (Vincennes University, starting 01-2027) is for transitioning service members, Guard/Reserve, veterans and dependents. No stipend. opnextjobs.com
  • America Makes (additive manufacturing institute): no direct paid training-to-hire program for individuals. Its tie to the Navy is through additive manufacturing workforce outreach and the Navy AM Center of Excellence, which shares the IALR campus with ATDM. For additive manufacturing training with a hiring pipeline, use ATDM's additive track.
  • BuildSubmarines.com (Navy/BlueForge Alliance): a national directory of "hundreds of training programs in all fifty states, many of them free." It runs the Talent Pipeline Program (recruit, hire, train and retain for one year) and NextOp's veteran pipeline. Job seekers can create profiles that SIB employers see.
  • BuildFreedom.US (Office of the Assistant Secretary of War for Industrial Base Policy, launched 07-2026): Cadenazzi testified on 09-04-2026 that it "aligns more than 70 Industrial Base Analysis & Sustainment (IBAS) workforce projects under one campaign." Launched in TX, MI and PA; expanded to OH, IL and WI. Per the War.gov release, Under Secretary Michael P. Duffey presented "a $10 million investment to skilled trades advocate Mike Rowe to support the mikeroweWORKS Foundation Scholarship program." The scholarships are not paid training.

F ApprenticeshipUSA / DOL registered apprenticeships

Ingalls (15 DOL-registered trades), the NNS Apprentice School (Commonwealth of Virginia registered), BIW (State of Maine certificate), the Navy public shipyards (DOL journeyworker certificates) and Fincantieri's Shipbuilder Welder program (Wisconsin DWD Bureau of Apprenticeship Standards) are all registered apprenticeships. They show up in the DOL apprenticeship finder. This is the federal route to defense-manufacturing apprenticeships.

Recommendations

  1. If you want the closest ATDM substitute and can relocate: apply to ATDM itself (attend the 11-11-2026 Discovery Day) and also apply directly to EB, NNS or Ingalls paid-trainee roles. Hiring at those yards is rolling. EB alone planned about 8,000 hires in 2026, including 3,500 at Quonset Point and 2,500 in Groton per Rayha's February memo (later revised to about 3,250 and 2,250).
  2. If you want the highest long-term value: aim for the spring 2027 Navy shipyard windows (Norfolk around March, Portsmouth around April–June, Pearl Harbor's ~10-day window) and the NNS/BIW/Ingalls apprentice schools. Set USAJOBS alerts now. Norfolk applicants need to schedule the Virginia Placement Test early.
  3. If you need cash during short training: choose BIW/CMCC ($500/week, Maine residents), Hanwha Philly ($150–$300/week, Philadelphia residents with welding experience) or Eastern CT MPI (attendance stipends plus support services).
  4. If you're a veteran or transitioning service member: add LIFT Operation Next (SkillBridge), NextOp via BuildSubmarines.com, and the federal-shipyard veteran preference.
  5. Before planning around a regional program: confirm it's still running. Several Navy-funded pilots (M3, Pathways to Shipbuilding) depend on grant cycles.

Caveats

  • Some details come from secondary sources: EB's $24/hr entry rate (education news site), Fincantieri pay ranges (job aggregators) and NNSY timing (inferred from past windows). Check against official postings.
  • "Stipend" in these programs usually means an attendance incentive or covered costs, not a living wage. Only the apprenticeship and trainee jobs pay full-time wages.
  • Most shipyard and federal programs require U.S. citizenship; TMA BlueTech's bootcamp is an exception.
  • The DoD is now styled the "Department of War" in 2026 official materials, so program names and URLs may use either term.
